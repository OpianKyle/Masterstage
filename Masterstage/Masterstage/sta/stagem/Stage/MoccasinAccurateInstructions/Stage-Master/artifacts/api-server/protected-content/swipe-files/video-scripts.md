# StageMaster — Video Lesson Scripts
## 7 high-impact scripts (~2.5 min each) — used for the narrated lessons, and yours to re-record in your own voice

> Every script is built on a named NLP (Neuro-Linguistic Programming) strategy — the same influence patterns used by elite coaches, negotiators and stage speakers. Each one opens with a pattern interrupt, installs one tool, and closes with a future-pace so the lesson sticks.

> Recording tips if you re-record: hit the first line HARD, look at the LENS, and let your energy run one notch hotter than feels natural. Where you see CAPS, punch the word. Where you see a line break, breathe. Around 400 words ≈ 2.5 minutes at a lively pace.

## Script 1 — Own the Room in Your Head (Module 1)

> NLP strategy: **The Reframe + The Confidence Anchor**

Stop. Right now, imagine going live in sixty seconds. Feel that? That tight chest, that drop in your stomach?

Good. Because that feeling is not fear. That is FUEL with the wrong label on it. And in the next two minutes I'm going to relabel it — permanently.

Here's what's actually happening. Your brain runs a two-hundred-thousand-year-old calculation: many eyes on me, possible judgment, DANGER. Your amygdala cannot tell the difference between a webinar and a wolf pack. So it floods you with adrenaline. Racing heart. Shaky voice. Blank mind.

Now here's the part that changes everything. Every speaker you admire feels that exact same flood. Every single one. The difference is one word — the word they attach to it. Amateurs say: I am terrified. Professionals say: I am ACTIVATED. Same chemistry. Different label. Completely different performance.

That's the first NLP tool in this course: the Reframe. You don't fight a feeling. You rename it. And the moment you rename it, your brain reorganises around the new name.

So say it out loud, right now: This is not nerves. This is READINESS.

Then we lock it in with tool two: the Confidence Anchor. Close your eyes and go back to a moment you were completely on fire — a conversation where you were funny, sharp, unstoppable. See what you saw. Hear what you heard. Turn it UP. Make it bigger, brighter, louder. And at the peak of that feeling — squeeze your thumb and middle finger together. Hold three seconds. Release.

Do that five times and you've built a switch. Squeeze those fingers before you hit record and your nervous system goes straight back to that state. That is not motivation. That is conditioning. It works whether you believe it or not.

Three shifts run underneath it all. Performer to GIVER — make it about them and panic has nowhere to live. Perfect to USEFUL — a stumble plus a great insight beats polished emptiness every time. Event to REP — every talk is training data, never a verdict.

Now do this today. Open the workbook, run the Fear Inventory, build your anchor, and record ONE sixty-second video. Nobody sees it. It just has to exist.

Because thirty days from now, you'll press record without thinking twice — and you'll trace it back to this rep. Go own the room in your head.

## Script 2 — Message Architecture (Module 2)

> NLP strategy: **Presupposition + Embedded Commands**

Five points. That's what killed your last talk. A talk with five points is a talk with ZERO points.

And when you finish this lesson — notice I said WHEN, not if — you'll be able to architect any talk in fifteen minutes flat.

Did you catch what I just did? That's the first NLP tool here: presupposition. I didn't ask whether you'd learn it. I embedded the outcome as already decided, and your brain quietly accepted it. Great speakers do this constantly. "As you start applying this..." "By the time you finish..." "The question isn't if this works — it's how fast."

You never argue someone into agreement. You presuppose the agreement and keep talking.

Now, tool two: embedded commands. Inside a normal sentence, you drop a direct instruction and mark it with your voice. "You can DOWNLOAD THIS TONIGHT and see the difference." "Some people REGISTER NOW, others wait." The conscious mind hears a sentence. The unconscious hears the command. Punch it slightly lower and slower — that's called analog marking, and it's how the instruction gets through.

Use this ethically. You mark commands for actions that genuinely serve them. That's the whole line.

Now the structure. One Core Idea, then four beats: Hook. Point. Proof. Payoff. H-P-P-P.

Your Core Idea is a CLAIM, not a topic. "Email marketing" is a topic. "Your list is the only audience you actually own" — that's a claim. Claims create tension. Topics create naps.

The Hook earns attention in thirty seconds. Stakes, story, contrarian claim, or question. What you never do is open with "hey guys, can everyone hear me." You have seconds — spend them.

The Point is your Core Idea, stated early and repeated. People join late and multitask. Repetition isn't lazy. It's DESIGN.

Proof comes in three flavours and you want one of each: a story, a pattern, a principle.

And the Payoff is one clear action — with an embedded command inside it.

Fifteen minutes: three on the Core Idea, four on the hook, five on proof, two on the payoff, one to say your open and close out loud.

So open the workbook and BUILD ONE TALK now, while this is hot. And as you write that Core Idea, you'll already feel the difference between talking — and architecting.

## Script 3 — Voice & Delivery (Module 3)

> NLP strategy: **Analog Marking + Auditory Pacing**

Listen to this: "This will change your business." Now listen again: "This… will change… your BUSINESS."

Same eight words. Completely different impact. That difference is worth money to you — and it lives entirely in your voice.

Here's why it matters more online than on any stage. In a room, the room carries you. On a stream, your voice does EIGHTY PERCENT of the work, and your viewer is one boring minute away from another tab. Monotone doesn't say your content is weak. It tells the listener's brain: nothing new is happening here. And attention obeys that signal instantly.

So we install the NLP tool that fixes it: analog marking. You mark your most important words with a change in sound, so the brain flags them as important. Slower. Lower. Louder. Or a full silence right before. Mark the Core Idea. Mark the command. Mark the price. Never mark the filler.

You've got four dials to mark with. Pace. Pause. Pitch. Power.

Dial one — PACE. Nervous speakers run at one-eighty words a minute and climbing. Sit at one-forty to one-sixty. But the real skill is variation: move fast through setup, then cut your speed in HALF on the line that matters. That contrast is the marking.

Dial two — PAUSE. The single most powerful sound in speaking is silence. Land your big point, then stop for three full seconds. It'll feel like a year. To them, it feels like confidence — and it's the moment the idea gets stored.

Dial three — PITCH. End your statements DOWN. Upward endings turn facts into questions and confidence into a request for permission. Lift your pitch inside stories, drop it on the claim.

Dial four — POWER. Volume comes from the belly, never the throat. Breathe low, speak on the exhale. Strain sounds like anxiety. Support sounds like authority.

And the second NLP layer is auditory pacing: match your listener's rhythm first, then lead them. Start near their energy, then take them somewhere. That's pace-and-lead, and it's why the best speakers never feel like they're shouting at you.

Two-minute warmup before every recording: five physiological sighs, thirty seconds of lip trills, thirty seconds of hums, then your opening line three different ways.

This week, pick ONE dial. Ninety-second reps daily. Review it audio-only with your eyes closed — and hear yourself becoming someone worth listening to.

## Script 4 — Camera Presence & Body Language (Module 4)

> NLP strategy: **Rapport Building + Sensory Acuity (Visual Pacing)**

Here's the brutal truth: your viewer decides whether to trust you before you finish your first sentence. Not from your words. From your FACE, your frame, and your eyes.

So let's fix all three — fast.

Start with the one-person principle. That lens is not an audience. It is ONE person, sitting close enough to touch. So you never say "hey guys" or "everyone watching." You say YOU. Singular. That single word swap is the fastest rapport-builder in online video.

Now the NLP tool: rapport through visual pacing. In person you build rapport by subtly matching posture, breathing and tempo. On camera you can't see them — so you match the state you WANT them in. Calm, warm, leaning slightly in, breathing slow. Because emotional states transfer through the screen. Mirror neurons don't care that it's a recording. Whatever you're feeling, they start feeling. If you're rushed, they feel rushed. If you're grounded, they settle.

That's why the fix for a bad take is never "try harder." It's change your state, then roll again.

Five frame settings, and they take four minutes. Lens at EYE LEVEL — never below, unless you enjoy filming your own ceiling. Eyes on the top third of frame. Sit an arm's length away, hands visible. Light in FRONT of you, never behind. And a background with a little depth — not a bare wall.

Then eye contact. When you deliver your Core Idea, your key line, or your call to action, you look straight into the LENS. Not the screen. Not your own face in the corner. The glass. That's what reads as "she's talking to me."

Hands next. Gesture inside the box — shoulders to waist, in frame. Count on your fingers. Open palms when you make an offer; open palms read as nothing to hide. Hidden hands read as hiding something.

And energy — camera eats about twenty percent of everything you bring. So start ONE notch hotter than feels natural. What feels slightly too much to you lands as normal and engaged to them.

Today: run the camera setup audit in the workbook, record sixty seconds straight into the lens, then watch it back with the SOUND OFF. That's the fastest feedback loop in this entire course — because you'll see exactly what they see, before you ever say a word.

## Script 5 — Storytelling That Sticks (Module 5)

> NLP strategy: **Nested Loops + Submodality Shifts**

A client of mine once froze on a live webinar. Nine hundred people watching. Dead silence for eleven seconds. I'll tell you how that ended — but not yet.

That? That's an NLP tool called an open loop. Your brain hates an unfinished story, so it stays online waiting for the close. Open a loop at the start of any talk, close it at the end, and your retention doubles. Stack two or three inside each other and you've got nested loops — the structure that makes people say "I couldn't stop watching."

So why do stories work when facts don't? Because facts trigger evaluation mode — defences up, comparing, arguing. Stories trigger SIMULATION mode. The listener runs your experience on their own hardware, feels it in their own body. And persuasion only ever happens in simulation mode.

Here's your template. Before. After. Bridge.

BEFORE — the struggle, in specific detail. Not "I was struggling." Try: "It was 2 a.m., I had four hundred rand in the account, and I was rewriting the same email for the ninth time." Specificity is what makes it real.

AFTER — the transformation, equally specific. Numbers. Dates. What changed.

BRIDGE — what caused the shift. And the bridge is where your point, your method, or your offer lives.

Now the second NLP tool: submodality shifts. Submodalities are the settings on a memory — brightness, size, volume, distance. When you narrate the BEFORE, use small, dark, tight, distant language: "everything felt narrow, quiet, far away." When you narrate the AFTER, go big, bright, loud, close: "suddenly the room was LOUD, the screen lit up, everything was right in front of me." You're not just telling a story. You're directing their internal cinema. That's the difference between a story people hear and a story people FEEL.

Bank four stories and you'll never be caught empty. The Founder story — why you do this. The Discovery story — how you found your method. The Customer story — one specific person's before and after. And the Failure story — the face-plant and what it taught you. Failure stories build more trust than any success story ever will.

Oh — and my client on that webinar? She breathed, said "give me a second, this matters," and closed forty-one thousand rand that day. The pause didn't cost her the sale. It WAS the sale.

Today: draft all four stories in Before-After-Bridge. Then tell one in your very next piece of content.

## Script 6 — Speaking to Sell (Module 6)

> NLP strategy: **Pace-and-Lead + The Yes-Set + Future Pacing**

Most people don't have a closing problem. They have a TRUST problem that only shows up at the close.

So let's fix the whole sequence — with the three most powerful persuasion patterns in NLP.

Pattern one: pace and lead. You pace by saying what's already true in their world. "You've tried the courses. You've posted consistently. And it still feels like shouting into a void." Three statements they silently agree with. Now they're with you — and only NOW do you lead: "Here's what actually changes it."

Never lead before you pace. That's why cold pitches bounce.

Pattern two: the yes-set. Stack small agreements before the big one. "You want more clients, right? You'd rather they came to you? And you'd rather not spend all day on social media?" Yes. Yes. Yes. Agreement is momentum — and momentum carries into the ask.

Pattern three: future pacing. Before you ever mention price, you walk them through owning it. "Picture yourself thirty days from now. You open your laptop, you hit record, and there's no knot in your stomach. You just… speak. What's that worth to your business?" You're letting them try on the result. People buy the picture long before they buy the product.

Now the structure — the eight-beat webinar spine. Hook and promise. Origin story. The Shift — what changed for you. The Framework — teach three REAL steps, honestly. Then the Turn. Then offer stack, objections, close.

The Turn is the hinge of the entire thing: "You can absolutely do this on your own — it took me four years — or, let me show you the shortcut." Say it SLOWLY. Then breathe.

Your pitch itself is sixty seconds. P-S-R. Problem, fifteen seconds. Solution, fifteen. Result plus a question, fifteen. Then you STOP TALKING. Amateurs keep selling past the yes. Professionals go silent and let the silence do the work.

And the ratio that makes all of it land: teach seventy percent, story twenty, offer ten. Every minute either deposits trust or withdraws it. That ten percent only converts because of the ninety that came first.

This week: map your offer onto the spine, script your Turn, write three objection answers, and drill your sixty-second pitch TEN times out loud.

Because the next time someone asks what you do — you'll already know exactly what to say.

## Script 7 — The 30-Day Practice System (Module 7)

> NLP strategy: **Future Pacing + The Swish Pattern + Identity-Level Change**

You now know more about speaking than ninety-five percent of the people in your market. And that's worth exactly NOTHING — until you turn it into reps.

So this is the lesson that decides whether the last six were entertainment or transformation.

Start with the NLP pattern that rewires the flinch: the Swish. Picture the image that makes you avoid recording — that dreaded picture of you freezing, or the comment you're scared of. See it. Now, in the corner of that picture, place a small bright image of the speaker you're becoming: relaxed, sharp, in flow. On the count of three, the small bright image EXPLODES forward and blasts the old one away. One. Two. THREE. Swish.

Now clear your mind and do it again. Faster. Five reps, and it takes about ninety seconds. What you're doing is training your brain to jump straight from trigger to the new picture. Do this every morning for a week and the flinch loses its grip.

Then three rules for the thirty days. Daily beats big — ten minutes every day beats three hours on Sunday. Record everything — the camera is the only honest coach you'll ever have. And one focus per rep — never fix four things at once.

Here's the map. Week one, Foundation: sixty-second reps daily, anchor before each one, baseline video on day seven. Week two, Voice: warmup plus a single dial, ninety-second reps. Week three, Camera and Story: ninety seconds straight to the lens, and all four stories drafted and told. Week four, Selling: pitch drills, then a mini-webinar to a tiny audience. Days twenty-eight and twenty-nine — you GO LIVE. Day thirty, you rewatch your baseline video next to today's, and you will not believe it's the same person.

And after every rep, log one win and one fix. Two lines. That's the whole journal.

Now the part that actually lasts. Stop saying "I'm working on my speaking." Start saying "I'm a speaker who's getting sharper." That's identity-level change, and identity is what survives the week you don't feel like it. Behaviour follows identity — always.

So print the tracker. Do Day One TODAY, not Monday.

Thirty days from now, you'll hit record without hesitating, you'll say exactly what you mean — and someone will buy because of how you said it.

The camera light is on. Go own it.
